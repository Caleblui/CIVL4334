0 · The recipe behind every calculation question

Read first · 2 minutes · applies to every guide below

Every Part C question, and every tutorial from Q3.1 to Q7.2, is the same comparison: can the member or joint carry the load? The guides below follow these six steps every time, so you always know which step you're on.

The general form of every strength check in AS1720.1:

\[ \underbrace{R_d}_{\text{design capacity}} = \underbrace{\phi}_{\text{reliability}} \times \underbrace{k_1 k_4 k_6 \ldots}_{\text{modification factors}} \times \underbrace{f'}_{\text{characteristic strength}} \times \underbrace{(A \text{ or } Z)}_{\text{section property}} \;\ge\; \underbrace{S^*}_{\text{design action}} \]

Two different checks, two different systems. Strength (ULS) uses factored loads, φ and k1. Deflection (SLS) uses unfactored loads × ψ, the creep factor j2 and the mean E, with no φ and no k1. Mixing the two is the most common way to lose marks.

1 · Load combinations and the critical case

Part C step 1 every year · Q3.1(a), Q3.2(a–b), Q3.4, Q4.4(a–b), 2025 P2(a)

The idea

Timber creeps and weakens under sustained load. A load that stays on for 50 years can only use 57% of the short-term strength. So the "worst" combination is not simply the biggest load: you divide each combination by its k1 to put them on the same footing.

Bar height = k1 for timber strength. Orange line = k1 for laterally loaded joints. Fasteners in withdrawal and steel strength always use k1 = 1.00 (Table 2.3 note).

The formulas

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{(a) Permanent only:}\quad & 1.35G & k_1 &= 0.57\\ \text{(b) Permanent + long-term live:}\quad & 1.2G + 1.5\,\psi_l Q & k_1 &= 0.57\\ \text{(c) Permanent + peak live:}\quad & 1.2G + 1.5Q & k_1 &= 0.80 \text{ (5 months) or } 0.94 \text{ (5 days)}\\ \text{(d) Wind uplift:}\quad & 0.9G + W_u & k_1 &= 1.00 \text{ (members)},\ 1.14 \text{ (joints)} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered}\text{Critical combination} = \max\left(\frac{N^*}{k_1}\right) \\[6pt] \text{then design with that combination's } N^* \text{ and its own } k_1\end{gathered} \]

3Divide and comparewhy: Eq 3.4(2) rearranged

2 · Choosing φ and every k factor

Part C step 3 every year · each factor is one yes/no question

Each factor answers one question about the situation. Write them in the same order every time, each with its source. That line alone earns method marks.

φ: first decide the category

Is the member primary in a post-disaster or essential-services structure (hospital, bridge with post-disaster function)?
Yes → Category 3 (Q3.1 bridge chord)
Is it a primary member in anything that is not a house, or a house element whose failure would affect more than 25 m²?
Yes → Category 2 (Q3.2 warehouse hanger, Q3.4 shopping-centre tie, Q4.4 column, Q5.3 beam, Q6.1 primary truss, 2025 P2 office stud)
Otherwise (house element affecting < 25 m², or any secondary member) → Category 1 (Q5.6 house joist, Q7.1 secondary truss)

Table 2.5 (least dimension → k₄): ≤ 38 mm 1.15 · 50 mm 1.10 · 75 mm 1.05 · ≥ 100 mm 1.00. Q3.1's 200 mm chord → 1.00.

k₇ bearing length (bearing checks only)

A short bearing spreads the load into the surrounding wood, so it can take a higher stress. Table 2.6: 12 → 1.75, 25 → 1.40, 50 → 1.20, 75 → 1.15, 125 → 1.10, ≥ 150 → 1.00. Between two rows, interpolate linearly:

\[ \begin{gathered}k_7 = k_{7,\text{low}} + (k_{7,\text{high}} - k_{7,\text{low}}) \frac{\ell - \ell_\text{low}}{\ell_\text{high} - \ell_\text{low}} \\[6pt] \begin{aligned}\text{Q5.3: } k_7 &= 1.15 + (1.10 - 1.15)\frac{105 - 75}{125 - 75} \\ &= 1.15 - 0.03 = 1.12\end{aligned}\end{gathered} \]

k₉ strength sharing (bending only, sawn timber only)

When several joists sit side by side under one floor, a weak joist sags a little and passes load to its neighbours. k9 credits that teamwork.

\[ k_9 = g_{31} + (g_{32} - g_{31})\left(1 - \frac{2s}{L}\right) \ge 1.0 \]

Brittle failure: what to write in Part B

  • Modes: splitting, row shear, block (group) tear-out, net tension, plug shear.
  • Causes: large stiff bolts that don't bend, tight spacing, short end distances, thick steel plates, fasteners on one grain line, shrinkage restrained by steel.
  • Prevent: larger end/edge distances and spacing along the grain; more, smaller fasteners that yield first; stagger fasteners; self-tapping screw reinforcement across the grain; seasoned timber or slotted holes; design so the steel yields first.

11 · Wood as a material: moisture, anatomy, grading, EWPs

Part A every year · Part B Q1

Moisture

  • MC = (m_wet − m_dry)/m_dry. It is on the dry mass, so MC can exceed 100%.
  • Fibre saturation point (~25–30%): cell walls full of bound water, no free water in the lumens. It can be found as the MC where shrinkage starts during drying.
  • Shrinkage and swelling only happen below FSP. Tangential ≈ 2 × radial; longitudinal is negligible.
  • EMC: the MC timber settles at in its environment. Seasoned = ≤ 15%, not 0%.
  • Hardwoods can take years to air-dry; softwoods are routinely kiln dried. Wide boards move more than narrow ones. Restraining movement causes cracks.

Anatomy

  • Softwoods (conifers): sap moves in tracheids, no vessels. Hardwoods (broadleaf, deciduous/leafy): vessels. Balsa is a hardwood.
  • From the centre: pith → heartwood → sapwood → cambium → bark. Directions: longitudinal, radial, tangential. Timber is orthotropic.
  • Wood dry weight is about 50% carbon. Decomposition or combustion releases it.

Grading

  • Small clears are defect-free specimens and stronger than structural timber. Knots, slope of grain, checks, wane, pith and size effect make sawn boards weaker.
  • Visual grading checks knots, grain angle, pith, wane, checks, gum veins. Not microfibril angle.
  • F-grades: hardwood and softwood. MGP: machine graded pine; the number ≈ E in GPa, not MoR. A-grades (A17): reliability-tested seasoned hardwood. GL: glulam.
  • Appearance grade ≠ stress grade. With several grade stamps, you may choose which one to use.
  • Bending strength (MoR) is not a true material property; it was introduced to simplify beam calculations.

12 · Durability, fire and bridges

Part A every year · Part B photo questions

Decay fungi

Need four things: moisture (MC > ~20%), oxygen, a suitable temperature, and food. Remove one and there's no decay. Submerged timber rarely rots (no oxygen). Hardwoods do decay.

Termites

  • Subterranean termites travel from the ground and don't need the timber to touch soil.
  • Defences: naturally resistant species (cypress), preservative treatment, physical barriers (mesh, ant caps, graded stone), chemical soil barriers, raised floors with inspection zones.
  • Paint doesn't stop termites. Thermal modification improves fungal resistance, not termite resistance.

Treatment

  • Match the treatment to the hazard class: H1 inside dry, H2 inside with termites, H3 outside above ground, H4–H5 in ground, H6 marine.
  • Sapwood is not durable but treats well. Hardwood heartwood resists treatment. Softwood heartwood also treats poorly.
  • Cut, drill and notch before pressure treatment, or you expose the untreated core (AS5100.9).
  • CCA and ACQ corrode fasteners. In-situ pole treatment: solid diffusible preservative rods.

Photo questions: what to say

  • Problems: exposed end grain, water traps (flat tops, bolts, plates), contact with concrete or ground, no roof protection, wet–dry cycling cracks.
  • Fixes: cap or flash end grain, extend the roof overhang, stand posts on a steel stirrup 75–150 mm clear, slope tops, gaps to dry, durable or treated species.
  • Decks: fewer face fixings (or hidden fixings) mean fewer holes for water to enter.

Fire

  • Timber does burn: heat drives off gases that ignite. The char layer insulates the wood below.
  • Char depth grows with time; charring rate depends on density.
  • Effective section = original − char depth − zero-strength layer (heated wood with no strength).
  • Heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation. Combustion is not one.
  • CLT self-extinction needs no char fall-off or delamination (heat-resistant adhesive) and limited exposed timber area.

Bridges (AS5100.9)

Unseasoned timber is not permitted. Load factor 1.25G (or 1.35G alone). Traffic counts as 5 months → k1 = 0.8. SLT, TCC and durable hardwood boards are common decks.
